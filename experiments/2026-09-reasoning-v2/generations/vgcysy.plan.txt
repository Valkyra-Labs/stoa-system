The **Order and emphasis** rule dominates, requiring the board to see the most critical deviations first. Exceptions and items needing urgent attention must appear before stable averages, ensuring the narrative starts with risk or opportunity. **Numbers** rule dictates that every metric shown for judgement includes its basis (target, previous period, or peer) and units, preventing ambiguous interpretations of "performance." **States** rule is crucial; the screen must explicitly handle empty or stale data to maintain trust, as a board expects complete and current information. Finally, **Evidence and actions** rule ensures that behind every trend line or metric, the underlying context (e.g., "term end," "peer group") is immediately visible, avoiding the need for secondary navigation during a presentation. These rules collectively force a layout that prioritizes clarity, context, and hierarchy over decorative density.
