1. **The person and the session**: The teacher opens this screen daily (or pre-emptively) to prepare for a specific meeting. It is a targeted, high-stakes task: understanding one student deeply to negotiate next steps with a parent.

2. **The shape of the work**: This is **understanding one object fully before acting on it**. The teacher needs a complete picture of the student's academic and behavioral context to formulate a coherent narrative for the parent.

3. **The information**: The view requires the student's current performance metrics, historical trends, specific incident logs, and relevant teacher notes. Values are judged against targets and previous periods to identify growth or decline.

4. **The decision and its evidence**: The teacher decides on the meeting's agenda and key talking points. They need evidence of recent behavior, specific data points, and the history of interventions to support their stance. A wrong decision (e.g., missing a trend) risks a failed meeting.

5. **The structure**: The screen must present the student's identity and current status prominently, followed by a detailed view of their metrics and history. A section for pending actions or notes allows the teacher to draft the meeting strategy before the meeting occurs.
