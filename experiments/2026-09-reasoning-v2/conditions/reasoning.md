# Reasoning before structure

Decide the structure of the screen from the work it supports before you
choose any component. Answer these questions in order; each answer
constrains the next.

1. The person and the session. Who opens this screen, how often, and
   what made them open it now? Is this a routine check, a response to
   something unusual, or a job they must finish?

2. The shape of the work. Which of these describes the session best?
   - getting a general picture of many unrelated numbers, with no
     action expected on this screen;
   - scanning a large collection again and again for the few items
     that deviate;
   - taking each item in turn to a decision, until none are left;
   - understanding one object fully before acting on it;
   - setting a few objects against each other.
   How many objects are in play, and does the person act on this
   screen or elsewhere?

3. The information. Which entities, measures and time frame must be in
   view together for that work? Against what is each value judged: a
   target, the previous period, a peer, a threshold? Which values
   change the person's next step, and which are background?

4. The decision and its evidence. What does the person decide here?
   What must they see beside the number to decide well: where it came
   from, how fresh it is, how certain it is, what happened before? What
   does a wrong decision cost, and can it be undone?

5. The structure. From the answers above, derive the regions of the
   screen and their order: what must be visible first, what can wait,
   where the detail of one item appears relative to the others, where
   progress through the work is shown, and where the action and its
   consequence sit. The structure should follow the shape of the work,
   not a familiar layout.

6. The components. Only now choose from the vocabulary the components
   that realise that structure. If a composed shell in the vocabulary
   matches the structure you derived, use it; if none does, compose
   the structure from smaller components.

Check the result against your answers: every region should serve a
question from steps 2 to 4, and nothing the person needs for the
decision should be hidden or a step away.

Before writing code, state in one comment at the top of the file your
short answers to steps 1 to 5.
