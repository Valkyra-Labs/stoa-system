# General interface guidance

Plan the screen before choosing components. Aim for a clear, consistent
and accessible interface that is easy to scan and pleasant to use.

1. Hierarchy. Decide what matters most and give it the most prominent
   place. Use headings, grouping and spacing to show how the parts
   relate. Keep the number of competing focal points low.
2. Consistency. Use components the same way everywhere. Keep labels,
   terminology and icons consistent. Align elements to a shared grid
   and keep spacing regular across the page.
3. Clarity. Prefer plain language in labels and headings. Make buttons
   say what they do. Keep forms short and group related fields.
4. Feedback. Show the state of the system: loading, empty, error and
   success states should each look intentional. Confirm important
   actions and make them reversible where possible.
5. Accessibility. Provide sufficient contrast, visible focus, keyboard
   access to every control, meaningful headings, and text alternatives
   for charts and images. Do not rely on colour alone.

Practical guidance
- Place the most important content near the top of the page.
- Use whitespace to separate groups instead of heavy borders.
- Keep tables readable: clear headers, aligned columns, and enough row
  height to scan comfortably.
- Use charts to show trends and tables to show details.
- Keep navigation predictable and visible.
- Limit the number of actions shown at once; group secondary actions.

Layout
- Start with the structure of the page: a header that names the screen,
  then the primary content, then supporting details.
- Group related information into clearly labelled sections, and order
  sections by how often they are used.
- Give each section one clear purpose; split a section that tries to do
  several things at once.
- Keep the density appropriate for the audience: professionals tolerate
  more information per screen than occasional visitors.
- Make empty states explain what will appear.
- Use progressive disclosure for rarely needed details, but keep the
  information needed for the main task visible.

Avoid
- Clutter: too many elements competing for attention.
- Inconsistent spacing, alignment or component usage.
- Long unbroken walls of text or numbers without grouping.
- Hidden or ambiguous controls.
- Decorative elements that do not support the content.
